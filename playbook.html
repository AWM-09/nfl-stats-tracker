Route tree: know the basics

Route names can vary between teams and playbooks. These are common beginner-friendly descriptions.

Routes begin at the line of scrimmage near the bottom and move upfield. Arrows show the receiver's path.

Go / Fly

Run straight upfield to stretch the defense vertically.

Slant

Take a few steps forward, then cut diagonally inside. Often used for quick throws.

Out / Curl

Out breaks toward the sideline; curl turns back toward the quarterback after moving upfield.

Dig / In

Push upfield, then break horizontally across the middle.

Post / Corner

Post breaks toward the goalpost; corner breaks toward the sideline/deep corner.

Comeback

Run deep, then turn back toward the sideline to present a target.

Formations & personnel

Shotgun: quarterback starts several yards behind the center, often giving more time to see the defense.

Under center: quarterback receives the snap directly from the center; common for quick play-action and traditional run looks.

2x2: two eligible receivers on each side. Trips: three receivers aligned to one side. Empty: no running back beside or behind the quarterback in the backfield.

Personnel: a number pair often describes running backs and tight ends, e.g. 11 personnel means one RB and one TE with three WRs.

Coverage basics

Man coverage: defenders are responsible for particular eligible players.

Zone coverage: defenders protect areas of the field and pass receivers between zones according to their rules.

Cover 2: typically two deep defenders. Cover 3: typically three deep zones. Cover 4: typically four deep zones.

Blitz: send extra rushers to pressure the quarterback, often leaving fewer defenders in coverage.

Run fit / gap: defenders have responsibilities to fill gaps and stop a run from getting through the front.

Read progressions & common concepts

Read progression

Quarterbacks learn where to look based on the concept and defensive reaction. A progression is a planned order of reads, but the quarterback must still react to pressure and coverage.

Spacing

Routes should spread defenders out, create clear throwing windows and avoid bringing multiple receivers into the same area.

Flood

Several routes attack different depths on one side of the field, making a zone defender choose which level to cover.

Mesh

Two crossing routes pass close to each other to create traffic and natural separation against some man coverages.

Play action

The offense initially sells a run before the quarterback looks to throw. It can influence linebackers, but takes time to develop.
